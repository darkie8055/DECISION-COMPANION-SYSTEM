## 🎯 The Decision-Making Process Explained

### Step 1: Choose Your Starting Point
**What:** Select a template or create a custom decision
**Why:** Templates save time with pre-built criteria
**Tips:**
- Use templates for common decisions (jobs, purchases, travel)
- Create custom when you have unique criteria
- You can always customize templates later

### Step 2: Define Your Criteria
**What:** List factors that matter for your decision
**Why:** Helps you think systematically about what's important
**Tips:**
- 3-7 criteria works best
- Be specific (not just "cost" but "upfront cost" or "monthly fees")
- Include both financial and non-financial factors

**Example (Job Decision):**
- Salary
- Work Culture
- Growth Opportunities
- Work-Life Balance
- Location

### Step 3: Set Importance Weights
**What:** Assign percentages to show how much each criterion matters
**Why:** Different factors are important for different people
**Tips:**
- All weights must add up to 100%
- Be realistic about your priorities
- You can test different weights later (sensitivity analysis)

**Example:**
- Salary: 40%
- Culture: 30%
- Growth: 20%
- Work-Life Balance: 10%

### Step 4: Rate Your Options
**What:** Score each option on each criterion (0-10 scale)
**Why:** Gives your gut feelings a numerical basis
**Tips:**
- 0-3: Doesn't meet criterion
- 4-6: Partially meets criterion
- 7-10: Excellently meets criterion
- Be objective, not aspirational

### Step 5: Analyze Results
**What:** See weighted scores and insights
**Why:** Helps you make data-driven decisions

**Tabs Explained:**
- **Analysis:** Weighted scores based on your priorities
- **Risk:** Potential problems with each option
- **Sensitivity:** How weight changes affect outcome
- **Comparison:** Side-by-side option comparison

## 💡 Pro Tips for Better Decisions

### 1. **Be Objective**
- Score based on facts, not feelings
- Avoid scoring your "favorite" option too high
- Be consistent with your scoring scale

### 2. **Test Different Weights**
- Sensitivity analysis shows what-if scenarios
- Helps you understand if your decision is robust
- Reveals hidden trade-offs

### 3. **Review Risk Assessment**
- Identifies weak points in each option
- Shows you what you might be giving up
- Helps you prepare for potential problems

### 4. **Consider Trade-offs**
- No perfect option usually exists
- Understand what you gain and lose
- Make conscious compromises

### 5. **Save and Revisit**
- Save your decisions for future reference
- Come back and adjust if priorities change
- Build a history of your decision-making

### 6. **Iterate When Needed**
- First analysis not satisfying?
- Adjust weights or scores and re-analyze
- Different weights often reveal new insights

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How do I know if I'm scoring correctly?
**A:** Scores should reflect reality, not your preference. If all options score 9-10, you're probably being too generous. Use the full 0-10 scale.

### Q: Can I change my decision after saving?
**A:** Yes! You can load any saved decision, adjust scores or weights, and re-analyze.

### Q: What if all options score similarly?
**A:** This means the options are very close. Review the sensitivity analysis to see what weight changes would matter. Or re-examine if your criteria are truly important.

### Q: Should I use more criteria for better decisions?
**A:** Not necessarily. 3-7 criteria is ideal. Too many criteria dilute what's important.

### Q: How do I know which tab to look at?
**A:** Start with Analysis for overall scores. Check Risk for potential problems. Use Sensitivity to understand what matters most.
